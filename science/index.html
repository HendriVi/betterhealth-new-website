Science & evidence

Clear about the evidence.
Clear about its limits.

A plausible explanation and an effective intervention are different questions. We separate what a model helps us understand from what research shows a method can achieve.

LearnScience & evidenceCourses & learningShort explainers
01

Stress & allostasis

The stress response helps the body adapt to demands. Repeated or prolonged activation can have cumulative costs. This gives us a framework for considering both demand and recovery.

02

Predictive processing

One theoretical account describes the brain as anticipating incoming information and bodily needs. It offers a way to think about expectations, learning and regulation; it does not establish a treatment on its own.

03

Measurement & feedback

Research in depression supports the value of structured measurement in particular treatment settings. We use the broader principle of defining a baseline, tracking meaningful change and revisiting decisions.

04

Workplace mental health

WHO guidance includes organisational changes alongside training and individual support. That is why our workshops address working conditions as well as personal skills.

How we communicate certainty

Evidence belongs to a method and an indication.

A label is a starting point. We also consider who was studied, the outcome measured, the quality of the research and its relevance to the individual question.

Established

Supported for the relevant use by clinical evidence or recognised practice guidance.

Conditional

Supported under particular conditions, for defined populations or indications.

Emerging

Promising findings with important uncertainty about effectiveness or generalisability.

Exploratory

Useful for generating questions. Conclusions and clinical decisions require additional support.
