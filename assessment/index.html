Assessment

Every measurement needs a useful question.

Your goals and history determine what to assess. Existing findings come first. Additional testing earns its place when it can change a decision.

For individualsOur approachAssessmentYour starting point
01 / Experience & everyday function

What changed, when did it start, and where does it interfere with life?

Structured conversation, relevant symptom measures and a review of previous assessments.

02 / Sleep & recovery

Is recovery limited by timing, duration, quality or a possible sleep disorder?

Sleep history, diary and selected additional investigations if clinically indicated.

03 / Cognition & attention

Which abilities are affected, and under which conditions?

Selected validated tasks, interpretation of everyday functioning and specialist assessment where appropriate.

04 / Physical contributors

Could an identifiable medical factor change the explanation or next step?

Medical history, examination and targeted laboratory or specialist investigation through the relevant clinician.

05 / Behaviour & environment

Which demands, routines and relationships sustain the current pattern?

A practical review of workload, activity, recovery opportunities and support.

06 / Specialist measurements

Would imaging or a physiological measurement answer a specific clinical question?

Availability, indication, limits and cost are discussed before any specialist testing is arranged.

What happens to the results?

Findings are read together with your symptoms, goals and history. The output is a ranked set of priorities, an explanation of uncertainty and a plan for what to do next.

A wearable score, single cortisol value or brain scan cannot establish burnout on its own. The meaning of a measurement depends on the question, method and context.
