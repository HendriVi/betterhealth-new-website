Burnout: what needs to change at work?

Understanding the experience is useful. Examining the conditions around it makes the conversation actionable.

Be precise about the term

WHO describes burnout as an occupational phenomenon associated with persistent workplace stress that has not been managed successfully. It involves exhaustion, greater distance or cynicism towards work, and reduced professional efficacy. [1]

Include the workplace

WHO guidance on mental health at work includes organisational interventions, manager and worker training, and individual support. [2] Our practical interpretation is to ask both what the person needs and what the organisation can change.

In a workshop scenario, imagine a team receiving three urgent requests from three different managers. A useful discussion starts with who sets the priority, which task can wait and how that decision is communicated. This gives the team something specific to work on.

Turn concern into a decision

For a team discussion, select one recurring situation and write down a proposed change, a responsible person and a review date. Useful prompts include:

  • Which expectation needs clarification?
  • What can be stopped, delayed or reassigned?
  • What would make it easier to raise a concern early?

These are discussion tools, not diagnostic criteria. An employee with persistent difficulties should have access to individual professional support; an educational workshop cannot determine the cause of their symptoms.

Sources & further reading

  1. WHO. Burn-out: an occupational phenomenon, 2019.
  2. WHO. Guidelines on mental health at work, 2022.
