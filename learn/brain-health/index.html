Brain health starts with everyday decisions

A practical way to approach long-term brain health: understand your relevant risks, then decide what is worth changing.

Think across the life course

The 2024 Lancet Commission describes multiple potentially modifiable risk factors for dementia across life. These include vascular and metabolic factors, hearing and vision loss, physical inactivity and social isolation. Population estimates do not predict an individual outcome. [1]

Start with the decisions

A useful prevention conversation starts with what is already known. Which relevant factors have been reviewed? Which findings would alter an action? What is feasible in everyday life? These questions help keep assessment connected to a purpose.

For example, someone may arrive with a new wearable score but no recent discussion of blood pressure or hearing. A structured review helps decide which issue deserves attention first. The order depends on the individual history and clinical context.

Prepare a focused conversation

  • Bring a concise history of relevant conditions, family history and previous results.
  • Describe what you want to preserve or improve in everyday function.
  • Ask what a proposed test would change in the plan.
  • Agree how progress will be reviewed.

Prevention is about improving the odds and addressing relevant risks. No assessment or programme can guarantee protection from cognitive decline. New or worsening cognitive symptoms require a clinical assessment.
