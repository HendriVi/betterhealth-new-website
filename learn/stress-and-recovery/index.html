Why switching off can feel so difficult

A short introduction to stress, allostasis and the difference between stopping work and recovering.

A response with a purpose

Stress involves coordinated changes that help us respond to demands. Attention, behaviour and bodily regulation shift with the situation. The response is adaptive; its duration and the opportunity to recover matter. [1]

Demand continues in context

Allostatic load describes the cumulative cost of repeated or prolonged adaptation. It is a scientific framework for thinking about sustained demands, rather than a diagnosis that can be read from one number. [1]

Consider a team member whose workday ends at six but whose priorities for tomorrow remain unclear. An evening routine may help create a transition. A conversation that clarifies priorities addresses a different part of the same situation. This is why context belongs in the discussion.

A useful reflection

As a reflection exercise, choose one recent demanding day. Describe what was asked of you, what remained unresolved and when you felt able to disengage. Keep observations separate from your explanation of them.

  • Which demand could be clarified or reduced?
  • Where could a recovery opportunity become more predictable?
  • Who would need to be involved for that change to work?

Use those observations as a starting point for a conversation. Persistent or worsening difficulties deserve a professional assessment that considers other possible contributors.
