Why this is hard

01

Advanced testing is available. Turning those results into a working, tailored plan is rare.

Bloodwork, brain imaging, cognitive testing, physiology, psychology, behaviour and lifestyle are often assessed and treated separately. The individual assessments may be high quality, but what is often missing is the structured process that connects the findings, determines what matters, translates them into a plan, and measures whether that plan worked.

  • 01

    Fragmented information

    Different tests and specialists produce isolated findings. Without a shared model it is difficult to see how the pieces interact or which findings actually matter.

  • 02

    Complex, interacting drivers

    Mental and brain health emerge from interactions between biology, psychology, behaviour and environment. Looking at one system in isolation can miss the mechanisms maintaining the current state.

  • 03

    Weak continuity

    An assessment is only a starting point. What changes over time is rarely compared against what was expected, so plans are continued or abandoned on impression rather than on evidence.

The BetterHealth model

02

One process, from what is happening to whether it changed

A diagnosis names a pattern. It leaves open what maintains that pattern in this person, at this point in time. We ask which biological, psychological, behavioural and environmental processes are holding the current state in place, which of them are modifiable, and whether changing them produces the response we predicted.

  1. 01

    Assess

    Clinical picture, physiology, behaviour, context.

  2. 02

    Interpret

    Findings read against each other, not in isolation.

  3. 03

    Prioritise

    Ranked by likely contribution and modifiability.

  4. 04

    Intervene

    The smallest effective step, with a stated expectation.

  5. 05

    Measure

    The outcomes that matter for this person.

  6. 06

    Update

    Compare prediction with outcome, revise the model.

The loop then runs again. Each pass narrows what is still uncertain.

The science behind the approach

03

Why a systems model follows from the evidence

Two ideas do most of the work.

The reasoning chain

  1. Psychiatric syndromes are heterogeneous.
  2. Similar symptoms can arise from different processes.
  3. Brain function depends on interacting systems.
  4. The brain predicts and regulates continuously.
  5. Biological state influences mental function.
  6. Experience and behaviour change physiology in return.
  7. Some variables can be measured reliably, some cannot.
  8. A measurement earns its place only when it can change a decision.
  9. Interventions should carry a stated expected change.
  10. Systematic re-measurement improves outcomes.

Evidence levels

04

Not everything we can measure is equally well established

We work with the best current scientific evidence, which includes both established methods and newer approaches where the evidence is promising and clinically relevant. For transparency, every method used in your assessment is labelled with where it stands, from established to exploratory.

Established

Supported by established clinical evidence and standard medical practice where indicated.

Conditional

Supported in particular populations, indications or circumstances, and applied only where those conditions hold.

Emerging

Scientifically promising, evidence still developing, conclusions kept cautious.

Exploratory

May generate hypotheses. Not presented with the certainty of established clinical medicine, and never the basis of a plan on its own.

How we decide what to test

06

Testing is useful only when it changes the intervention

Blanket testing produces large volumes of low-value data.

  1. 01

    Question

    What are we trying to clarify clinically?

  2. 02

    Uncertainty

    Which unknown could change the decision?

  3. 03

    Measurement

    What test, marker or observation answers that question?

  4. 04

    Action

    What changes if the result is abnormal, normal or unchanged?

Our rule

If a test cannot plausibly change the decision, it is not part of the roadmap.

From data to decision

07

We do not treat every abnormal value. We build one working model.

Findings are weighed against your symptoms, function, goals, history, current state, risk, constraints and preferences.

  1. 01

    Interpretation

    Each finding is read in the context of the others and of your clinical picture, rather than against a reference range alone.

  2. 02

    Ranked hypotheses

    Competing explanations for what maintains the current state are listed and then ranked. The ranking is the clinical work.

  3. 03

    Prioritised action

    The first step targets the highest-ranked driver that is modifiable and fast to read out, so the model is tested in weeks rather than months.

Intervention

09

Intervention follows the formulation, and stays inside medicine

What we do depends on what the assessment found and on what is most likely to move.

  • Psychiatric and psychotherapeutic treatment

    Diagnosis, medication and its optimisation, psychotherapy, risk assessment.

    Established

  • Sleep, activity, nutrition and recovery

    Sleep and circadian intervention, conditioning, nutrition, behavioural change, stress regulation.

    Established Conditional

  • Medical contributors and specialist pathways

    Identified medical contributors are treated through the appropriate medical route.

    Established

  • Selected supplementation

    Only where a deficiency or specific indication has been demonstrated.

    Conditional

  • Neuromodulation and neurofeedback

    Considered in specific presentations. The evidence base varies considerably by method and indication.

    Emerging

  • Coordination

    Holding the model together across everyone involved.

    Conditional

Measuring whether it worked

10

Treatment is not assumed to work because it was delivered

Before anything starts, we state what should change, by roughly when, and how it will be measured.

  1. 01

    Baseline

    Symptoms, function and the relevant measures, recorded before anything changes.

  2. 02

    Intervention

    One clearly defined step, chosen for expected effect and feasibility.

  3. 03

    Expected response

    What should change, in what direction, by roughly when. Stated in advance.

  4. 04

    Reassessment

    The same measures under the same conditions.

  5. 05

    Comparison

    Prediction against outcome. A miss is information, not failure.

  6. 06

    Adjustment

    Continue, modify, or revise the model and rank the hypotheses again.

Outcomes are chosen for the individual.

Who this is for

11

Four situations this is built for

Not everyone needs every assessment, and not everyone needs us.

Mental health with incomplete response

You have a diagnosis and have been treated, and the response has been partial, absent, or bought at a cost in function.

The question we would work on: what is maintaining this, that treatment has not yet addressed?

Cognition and sustained performance

Concentration, cognitive capacity, stress tolerance or recovery are not where they were.

The question we would work on: is this a capacity problem, a state problem, or a demand problem?

Brain health and prevention

You want to understand which factors associated with long-term brain and cognitive health apply to you.

The question we would work on: what is your actual modifiable risk, separated from what is merely measurable?

Complex or unsynthesised cases

Multiple interacting symptoms, several specialists, a stack of reports, and no single model.

The question we would work on: what do these findings look like when read together?
