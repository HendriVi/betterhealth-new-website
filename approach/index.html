Our approach

From more information to better decisions.

Biology, psychology, behaviour and environment continuously influence one another. Understanding their relationships helps determine where to start.

For individualsOur approachAssessmentYour starting point
01

Assess

Begin with the experience: symptoms, function, chronology, goals and constraints. Existing reports are considered in context.

02

Interpret

Consider possible explanations and how the findings relate. A diagnosis describes a syndrome; it may leave open what maintains it in this person.

03

Prioritise

Rank the factors by likely relevance, uncertainty and whether a feasible change could make a difference.

04

Act

Agree an appropriate intervention, who is responsible and what change is expected.

05

Measure

Track the outcomes that matter: daily function, symptoms or relevant measurements under comparable conditions.

06

Update

Compare the observed response with the expectation. Continue, change the plan or revisit the explanation.

Four connected domains

The person is the whole system.

The same complaint can have different contributing factors. The purpose of this model is to organise useful questions.

Brain & psychology

Attention, mood, expectations, learning, symptoms and cognitive function.

Body & physiology

Sleep, metabolic health, medical history and physiological contributors where indicated.

Behaviour & habits

Movement, recovery, routines, avoidance, workload and the behaviours that maintain a pattern.

Environment & relationships

Demand, control, uncertainty, social support and the setting in which change has to happen.

A framework for asking better questions.

Predictive processing and allostasis help explain how the brain anticipates demands and regulates the body. They guide questions, while evidence for a particular assessment or intervention must be judged separately.
